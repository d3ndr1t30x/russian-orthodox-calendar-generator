# Reference calendar analysis

## Authority and scope

The uploaded files are design references only. Their saints, feasts, ranks, fasts,
holidays, and dates are not imported by the application. The application data
pipeline remains the sole content source.

The DOCX is the intended publication design. It is a 24-page bilingual calendar:
each month is followed by the equivalent Russian page. The supplied PDF is a
12-page, A4 portrait export made by an earlier version of this application and
does not share the DOCX's table geometry, typography, legends, or information
hierarchy. It is retained as a baseline comparison, not as the target design.

## Physical geometry

- A4 landscape: 297 x 210 mm (841.89 x 595.28 pt).
- DOCX margins: 7.1 mm left/right, 5.1 mm top/bottom.
- Printable table width: 283.0 mm (16,044 twentieths of a point).
- Seven equal columns: 40.43 mm each.
- Sunday-first ordering.
- A narrow weekday colour strip precedes the publication body.
- Month geometry varies between four, five, and six week rows; unused cells are
  merged or visually grouped for month titles and compact legends.
- Borders are black, approximately 0.45-0.65 pt, with no inter-cell spacing.
- Cell padding is approximately 1.7-2.1 mm.

## Typography

The DOCX body is overwhelmingly Arial Narrow. Large month names use a high-
contrast serif face. The application uses embedded Noto Sans and Noto Serif as
redistributable, Cyrillic-complete substitutes. Gregorian dates are large serif
figures; Old Style dates are smaller serif figures on the same baseline. Sunday
dates are red. Sunday headings and principal observances are bold condensed sans.
Major feast names are red, centred, and prominent. Russian source strings remain
Russian and are not generated by translating English strings.

## Colour and hierarchy

- Sunday weekday strip: dark red.
- Monday-Saturday weekday strips: vivid blue.
- Normal cells: white.
- Strict fast cells: medium neutral grey.
- Great Feast/Vigil cells: pale pink with red date and feast lettering.
- Rank/service emphasis: red text and a small source-derived rank glyph.
- Fasting permissions: small blue/gold bundled glyphs.
- Australian holidays: blue text, visually separate from liturgical content.
- School/user annotations in the reference are green; the application reserves
  this role for non-liturgical annotations.

## Cell anatomy

1. Large Gregorian day number at upper left.
2. Small Old Style day number immediately to its right.
3. Rank icon at the upper right.
4. Small fasting permission icons beside/below the rank icon.
5. Sunday/major liturgical heading.
6. Feast and selected saint lines as separate components.
7. Fasting label when useful.
8. Australian public holiday at the bottom in blue.

Overflow is line-clamped with an explicit additional-items indicator. It never
overprints the bottom annotation area.

## Legends and icons

The DOCX integrates fasting and Typikon-sign legends into unused calendar cells,
instead of appending a large footer. Glyphs are compact and aligned to the text
baseline. The application follows this pattern where blank cells are available
and uses a compact in-grid legend band otherwise. Rank and fasting glyphs remain
independent data/visual systems.

## PDF versus DOCX

The supplied PDF is A4 portrait with Monday-first columns, a beige masthead,
small sans-serif dates, abbreviated source prefixes, a bottom source footer, and
many empty rows. The DOCX is A4 landscape, Sunday-first, far denser, bilingual by
paired pages, uses large civil/Old Style figures, coloured weekday tabs, in-grid
legends, and publication-style feast treatments. Because the PDF identifies
itself as an older application export while the DOCX is the edited publication,
the DOCX is the target for this pass. The PDF remains useful for checking that
dynamic data, provenance, and annual pagination survive the redesign.
